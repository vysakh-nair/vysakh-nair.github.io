Complex markets — Moxie

How do you make crypto understandable?

Moxie let people launch, discover and trade creator tokens. The idea was simple; the system underneath wasn't. With the founders and engineering, I designed the product end to end as it moved from Farcaster Frames to an in-house platform to AI-powered trading. The goal: people understand the outcome without having to understand the infrastructure.

The problem

Crypto asked people to understand the system before they could take part.

A creator wanted to launch something for their community. A supporter wanted to back a creator they believed in. Underneath both sat a chain of infrastructure:

Wallet→ Token→ Transaction→ Price→ Liquidity→ DEX
The value proposition was social. The interaction model was financial.

Decision 01 · Distribution

Start where discovery already happens.

We built inside Farcaster Frames: see a creator, view the token, buy and confirm without leaving the feed.

Result and trade-off

10,000+ launches and $5M+ onchain volume proved the idea. The cost: button-driven journeys of 5–8 states, mostly forward and back, with nowhere to hold context. That limit set up the next decision.

Decision 02 · Language

Don't teach crypto. Translate it.

Moving in-house gave us control of navigation, discovery, trading and market data. The principle: neither hide the complexity nor expose it raw. Reveal it progressively.

Mechanics as plain questions

Every concept answered in the user's words before its technical name.

Bonding curve→How does the price change?
Market cap→How large is this economy?
Liquidity→How easily can I trade this?
Gas→What will this transaction cost?
Transaction→Did my purchase go through?
Close-up
  1. The curve is a shape you pick, not a formula you configure.
  2. Volatility is relative: each preset says how much wilder it is than the others.
  3. Names carry the tone of the community, from Conservative to Supa Based.

Decision 03 · Trading

Make a trade a decision, not a transaction builder.

One screen answers what am I buying, what am I paying, what will I get, and what happens next.

5–8 → 1–3sequential states became primary screens
Close-up
  1. Every state has its own word and colour: open, filled, expired, cancelled.
  2. Time is concrete: a countdown while open, a timestamp once filled.
  3. Progress is visible at a glance with a fill bar, not a number to decode.
Then: ask instead of navigate

Search, inspect, configure, trade became ask, understand, act. For anything consequential, people still saw what the agent understood, what it would cost and what they would get before it executed.

Outcomes

10K+creator token launches
$5M+onchain volume via Frames
~$25MMoxie market cap

A constrained social experiment became a general interface for onchain markets, from creator tokens to trading across Base assets.

What I learned

Good crypto UX doesn't hide complexity. It puts it where it belongs.
