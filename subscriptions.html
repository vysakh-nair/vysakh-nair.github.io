Business model — Senpi

What makes someone pay for an AI quant?

In October 2026 Senpi stopped charging a fee on trades and became a pure subscription business. That changed the job of every screen: it now had to turn a trader into a subscriber. I designed how the change met users, then rebuilt the first week around the moments people actually pay.

01 — The bet

A fee on every trade made Senpi look like one more trading app.

Senpi earned from a fee on every trade and from subscriptions to its AI. The fee was small, about $3,060 a month. The signal it sent was not: the professionals we wanted most don't trade through apps that take a cut.

The professionals

84%

of Hyperliquid volume outside market makers carries no app fee at all. Serious traders go direct and pay the exchange only.

Our best users

$16 vs $128

a month in fees against what they paid us in plans and credits. Noise to them. The two high-volume users who did notice it left.

The traders

90 → 131

users net positive over 90 days, re-priced at zero: 46% more. Users ahead after their first month were likelier to still be trading in month four: 31% against 21.5%.

The scale

10×

At 10,000 subscribers, subscriptions bring in about ten times what the fee ever could.

Options weighed
A lower rate→Still a line on a desk's statement. Professionals need zero, not low.
Rates that fall by plan→Worth about $4.50 a month to a typical trader. Complexity for nothing.
A new no-fee plan→The riskiest: the plan below it already renewed at only 54%.
Zero for everyone→Removes the reason to subscribe.
Chosen→0% on every paid plan, 0.03% without one. Plans climb on capacity, never on fees.

The founders made the call. I designed how it met users, and what the product did next.

02 — The launch

Say 0% once, loudly. Then let plans sell capacity.

One announcement where intent is high, one quiet banner everywhere else, and plans rewritten so the fee is a line item, not the pitch.

  1. One number does the work. 0%, then the comparison that makes it mean something: MetaMask, Phantom, Rabby.
  2. Honest small print. “Free trial trades carry a 0.03% Senpi fee” sits on the modal, not behind a link.
  3. Two clear exits. Subscribe for 0%, or continue the trial. No dark patterns.
  1. Plans climb on capacity. Credits, strategies running at once, new strategies a cycle.
  2. Usage in one glance, one tap from the composer, so the next plan shows up as a need, not an ad.
  3. Top up in place, without leaving the session.

Share who paid. A correlation: engaged people both fund and pay. We designed for the pattern, not a proven cause.

Three moments tip people

The trial ending while money is running. Running out of credits. Checkout at signup, which converts on intent but activates poorly: of 9 such buyers, 3 cancelled and 4 never traded.

Some starts lead nowhere

0% of people who opened with “Scan Senpi Signals” or “Fund my wallet” paid. 75 of 84 wallets added at signup had no Hyperliquid trading to read.

One start wins at volume

“Help me choose a trading strategy” converts best of all the starters people actually use: 3.9% of 486.

Connectors mark the power users

27% of people who connect Claude or ChatGPT pay. They buy 91% of top-ups, and come back through their own AI every day.

05 — What didn't go to plan

Strategy limits pushed too hard, twice.

Limits were meant to move people up a plan. In two places they pushed people away instead, and we walked both back.

Existing subscribers

Changing the deal mid-subscription

  1. The bet

    Apply the new limits at their next renewal, so they'd upgrade to keep what they had.

  2. What happened

    They cancelled instead.

  3. What we did

    Grandfathered everyone already paying. Limits apply to new subscribers only.

New subscribers

A warning louder than the problem

  1. The bet

    A limit banner above the chat composer, the slot credit alerts already use.

  2. What happened

    It alarmed people more than it should have. Running out of credits stops the agent. Reaching a strategy limit doesn't: the chat still works.

  3. What we did

    No banner. The usage ring by the composer turns red, and one tap shows what's used. The agent knows the limits: try to create a strategy past yours and it says so, and offers the next plan. The upgrade prompt lives in the plan usage card.

Before

The credit wall's red banner, for a limit that doesn't stop the chat.

After · Composer

Just a red ring. Tap it for usage; the chat stays clear.

After · Plan usage

The upgrade prompt lives where people check their plan.

A limit should speak up only when it stops you.

Results

Early, and honest about it.

The two days after launch brought 7 new customers, against an October average of 1.7 a day. In-app purchases went live the same days, so the two can't be separated yet.
